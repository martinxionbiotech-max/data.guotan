what_it_measures: >-
  Burning time describes how long a specified quantity of charcoal remains alight
  and usable: from established ignition until the ember is no longer hot enough
  for the intended use, or in a hookah context until the bowl is spent. It is a
  duration, so it always has two ends that must be defined — the ignition
  condition at the start, and the endpoint at the finish.
why_it_matters: >-
  For hookah and grill users, burning time is the most directly felt performance
  attribute, because it sets how often charcoal has to be changed and therefore
  the cost-in-use per session. Two products with similar ash and fixed carbon can
  deliver very different session economics if one holds its heat considerably
  longer.
typical_method: >-
  There is no single universal standard burning-time method for charcoal. Time-to-
  ash-over and total burn duration are usually measured in-house on a defined rig:
  a fixed mass or number of pieces, a defined ignition source and ignition time,
  stated airflow or ventilation, a fixed distance to the heat target, a defined
  endpoint (for example first ash-over, loss of glow, or falling below a heat
  threshold), and controlled ambient temperature and humidity. Because the set-up
  is defined per laboratory, a burning-time figure is only meaningful when the
  full test conditions travel with it.
buyer_relevance: >-
  Ask for the test set-up, not just the number: piece size or mass, ignition
  method, airflow and ventilation, vessel or bowl geometry, ambient temperature
  and humidity, and how the endpoint is defined. A supplier testing larger pieces
  in still air will report longer times than one testing small pieces in a draft;
  without the conditions, the two figures are not comparable and cannot be
  converted into a per-session cost.
limitations: >-
  Burning time varies strongly with size and shape — larger pieces and denser
  briquettes generally burn longer — as well as with airflow, how the pieces are
  stacked, and the surrounding equipment. Ambient humidity and the dryness of the
  batch also matter, and the same charcoal can behave differently on a second day.
  No laboratory figure can reproduce the exact conditions of a buyer's bowl, grill
  or oven, so treat burning time as a ranked indicator rather than an exact
  prediction.

## Why size and shape dominate

Burning time is downstream of physical form more than of chemistry. Surface-to-
volume ratio, packing density and airflow move the number more than small
differences in [fixed carbon](/specifications/fixed-carbon/) or
[volatile matter](/specifications/volatile-matter/). This is why
[size tolerance](/specifications/size-tolerance/) and
[ash content](/specifications/ash-content/) are usually read alongside it.

## What a buyer should ask for

- The full test set-up, not just a duration.
- Whether the test used a bowl, a grill or a free-standing rig.
- Number of pieces and mass used, and the endpoint definition.
- Whether the number is a single run or an average of repeats.
