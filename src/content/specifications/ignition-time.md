  Ignition time describes how long a charcoal takes to go from cold to fully
  lit — a uniform, usable glow across the piece — once an ignition source is
  applied. It has two ends that must be defined: the ignition source at the
  start (for example a coil burner at a stated setting) and the endpoint at the
  finish (full uniform glow versus "usable in session").
why_it_matters: >-
  Ignition time is a workflow and experience metric. For a hookah lounge it sets
  how long staff wait before a bowl can be served; for a retail or home user it
  shapes the lighting routine. It also distinguishes the two product families:
  natural coconut charcoal, which needs an external heat source and typically
  takes several minutes, versus quick-light charcoal, which carries an accelerant
  layer and ignites in under a minute. That distinction is a product decision,
  not just a convenience detail, because the accelerant layer is a known source
  of taste and emission complaints.
typical_method: >-
  There is no single universal standard for charcoal ignition time; like burning
  time it is a configuration-specific measurement. A defined piece size or mass
  is placed on a defined heat source — commonly an electric coil burner at a
  stated power — and the time is recorded from ignition start to a defined
  endpoint (full glow, or first usable state). The setup must specify piece size,
  number of pieces, heat source and its setting, ambient conditions and the
  endpoint definition, because all of them move the result. Natural charcoal and
  quick-light charcoal are effectively tested to different mechanisms, so their
  ignition times are not directly comparable.

  source and endpoint when comparing products, and should reproduce the test on

  damper pieces take longer — so ignition time is reviewed alongside

  prediction.

## Where ignition time sits among the metrics

Ignition time is downstream of physical form and moisture more than of chemistry:
a larger, denser or damper piece lights more slowly. It is the natural companion
to [burning time](/specifications/burning-time/) — one measures the start of the
session, the other its length — and both are read against
[volatile matter](/specifications/volatile-matter/), since higher volatiles
light faster but smoke more.

## What a buyer should ask for

- The product type (natural versus quick-light) and its trade-offs.
- The heat source and power setting used for any reported figure.
- The piece size and the moisture figure, since both move ignition time.
- A sample for a controlled lighting test on the buyer's own burner.
