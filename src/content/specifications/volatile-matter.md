what_it_measures: >-
  Volatile matter is the fraction of the fuel that is driven off as gas or vapour
  when the sample is heated to a high temperature without air. It is the
  quick-releasing part of the fuel, as opposed to the fixed carbon that burns as a
  glowing ember.
why_it_matters: >-
  Volatile matter governs how readily charcoal ignites and how much flame and
  smoke it produces in the early phase. More volatiles generally means faster
  lighting and a livelier flame, but also more start-up smoke and odour and a
  faster release of the fuel's energy. Lower volatiles typically give a steadier,
  cleaner ember with less start-up smoke. For hookah use, where the aim is a
  clean, low-odour heat source, volatile content is tied directly to perceived
  smoke and taste.
typical_method: >-
  A weighed test portion in a covered crucible — covered so that air is excluded —
  is heated at a specified elevated temperature for a specified time, then cooled
  and reweighed. The mass loss, corrected for the moisture already determined, is
  the volatile matter. This is part of proximate analysis: the ASTM D1762-style
  approach for wood charcoal, and the general solid-biofuel method ISO 18123.
  Because the result is defined by the crucible fit and the temperature/time
  programme, figures depend on the method used.
buyer_relevance: >-
  Ask how the supplier balances ignition behaviour against smoke: a fast-lighting
  charcoal is often the higher-volatile product, while clean-burn or low-odour
  lines trend towards less volatile matter. Require the moisture and ash figures
  alongside the volatile figure, since volatile matter is only meaningful when the
  whole [proximate set](/specifications/fixed-carbon/) is reported on one basis.
limitations: >-
  Volatile matter is method-sensitive — temperature, hold time, crucible fit and
  sample size all shift the result — and it does not capture the behaviour of
  binders or additives in briquettes, which also release gases on heating. It is a
  laboratory fraction, not a direct measurement of what a user smells or sees in a
  specific bowl.

## Ignition versus endurance

Volatile matter and [fixed carbon](/specifications/fixed-carbon/) sit at opposite
ends of the ignition/endurance trade-off: volatiles light fast and burn bright,
fixed carbon holds heat and lasts. Both are read against
[burning time](/specifications/burning-time/) when a buyer needs a specific
session length.

## What a buyer should ask for

- The volatile-matter figure and the method used to obtain it.
- The companion moisture and ash figures, on the same basis.
- Whether the product contains binders or additives, and how they behave on heat.
