what_it_measures: >-
  Size tolerance describes how close real pieces are to the size they are sold as.
  A nominal size class such as a 25 mm or 26 mm cube is a target; individual
  pieces in a commercial batch will vary, and the tolerance is the range of
  variation the buyer accepts and the producer can hold in routine production.
why_it_matters: >-
  Uniformity drives both packaging and burn behaviour. Pieces that are close in
  size pack predictably, fill a fixed-volume carton consistently, and burn at a
  similar rate so that a bowl or grill heats evenly. Wide variation means
  undersized pieces burn out while oversized pieces are still going, and cartons
  may over- or under-fill against the stated count or weight.
typical_method: >-
  Dimensional measurement: a random sample of pieces is measured with calibrated
  calipers or a go/no-go gauge across the relevant dimension — edge length for
  cubes, diameter for round or granular product — and the distribution (mean,
  spread and range) is compared with the nominal size class. There is no universal
  charcoal-specific standard for this; producers and buyers agree the nominal size
  class, the sampling plan and the acceptable deviation in the purchase
  specification, and record it there.
buyer_relevance: >-
  Define the nominal size and the tolerance in writing before ordering, and state
  the sampling plan (how many pieces, measured how). Ask for a measurement summary
  — mean and range — rather than a single "25 mm" claim. Tolerance is also a
  packaging issue: if pieces are specified by count per carton, tolerance
  determines whether the stated count and the stated weight can both be true.
limitations: >-
  Tolerance measured at the factory is a snapshot of one sample; breakage and
  moisture change during transport can shift the distribution by the time goods
  arrive. Measurement results also depend on how pieces are measured (which
  dimension, calipers versus gauge, dry versus dusty) and on how the sample was
  drawn, so the sampling plan must be stated for figures to be comparable. Very
  tight tolerances raise cost, so the practical tolerance is a commercial
  trade-off, not a physical constant.

## Tolerance, packing and burn

Size tolerance sits between the physical product and two commercial outcomes:
carton fill (count and weight agreeing) and even burning. It is normally reviewed
together with [burning time](/specifications/burning-time/) and
[ash content](/specifications/ash-content/), since residue and burn rate are felt
most when pieces are uneven.

## What a buyer should ask for

- The nominal size class and the permitted deviation, in the purchase spec.
- The sampling plan and the measuring instrument used.
- A mean-and-range summary from a random sample, not a single claimed size.
- An arrival check if the pieces are packed in a way that can abrade.
